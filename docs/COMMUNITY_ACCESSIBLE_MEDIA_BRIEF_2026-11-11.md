# IWACU RWANDA — Community-reviewed accessible media brief
**Date:** 2026-11-11  
**Working branch:** `feature/accessibility-inclusion-20261111`  
**Status:** Preparation only. No unreviewed sign-language or synthetic Kinyarwanda media is published.

## Purpose

Prepare reliable media support for residents who prefer or need Rwanda Sign Language (RSL) or spoken Kinyarwanda guidance. The site must never invent signs, mislabel another sign language as RSL, or use an unrelated synthetic voice as if it were Kinyarwanda.

The existing photo-led IWACU design stays in place. Any future media is optional and appears only when the visitor chooses to open it.

## Rwanda Sign Language (RSL) video production

### Required community review
1. Identify Deaf RSL users and fluent RSL contributors in Rwanda, including people from different age groups and regions where possible.
2. Agree on the exact meaning and natural signing for each instruction before filming.
3. Record the names/roles of reviewers, the date, consent, permitted uses, and any restrictions for each clip.
4. Ask at least two independent fluent Deaf RSL reviewers to review each finished clip. Resolve disagreements before publishing.
5. Never use AI-generated signing, invented gestures, or another country's sign language as a substitute for RSL.

### Initial clips to prepare
1. **Welcome and choosing a language:** show where the language selector and accessibility settings are.
2. **Find my location:** explain the “Menya aho uri” action and the phone's location permission.
3. **Correct the location:** explain the village list and that the map is optional.
4. **Find local leaders:** show the location result, the leader's role, and the call/contact action.
5. **Ask Iwacu AI:** show how to type or use the microphone, review a transcript, and send it.
6. **Emergency assistance:** show the emergency strip, the verified 112 call action, and the offline emergency list.
7. **Privacy and permissions:** explain when location and microphone permissions can appear and how to decline them.

### Recording and publishing checklist
- Use a real RSL signer who has agreed to appear and approved the final edit.
- Keep hands, face, torso, and facial expression clearly visible on a phone screen.
- Use even lighting, a plain uncluttered background, a stable camera, and framing that does not crop signing space.
- Do not place captions, decorative shapes, or overlays over the hands or face.
- Provide a Kinyarwanda written transcript and clear, plain-language steps beside the clip.
- Provide captions or text for any spoken voice-over; voice-over is supplementary, not the main message.
- Offer pause, replay, and stop controls. Never autoplay video or sound.
- Add descriptive title, language metadata (`rw-RW` for the written Kinyarwanda companion and `rsl-RW` as a project label only after language metadata is verified), duration, consent record, and reviewer approval.
- Compress videos for mobile delivery, but do not lower quality so far that handshape or facial expression becomes unclear.
- Keep a poster/thumbnail and the written instructions available when the clip cannot load.

## Human-recorded Kinyarwanda audio guidance

### Recording scripts for community review

These are draft scripts, not recordings. A fluent Kinyarwanda speaker and people who have difficulty reading should review the wording and pacing before recording.

**1. Welcome**
“Muraho. Murakaza neza kuri IWACU RWANDA. Hano ushobora kumenya aho uri, kubona abayobozi baho, no kubaza Iwacu AI.”

**2. Find location**
“Kanda kuri Menya aho uri. Telefone ishobora kukubaza niba wemera ko urubuga rumenya aho uri. Wemere gusa niba ubishaka.”

**3. Correct location**
“Niba aho ugaragajwe atari ho uri, fungura uburyo bwo guhitamo umudugudu. Ushobora gukoresha urutonde. Ntugomba gukoresha ikarita.”

**4. Find local leaders**
“Umaze kumenya aho uri, reba abayobozi b'aho hantu. Soma amazina n'imyanya yabo, hanyuma uhitemo guhamagara cyangwa ubundi buryo bwo kubandikira buriho.”

**5. Ask Iwacu AI**
“Ushobora kwandika ikibazo cyangwa gukanda Vuga ikibazo igihe telefone yawe ibishyigikira. Amagambo telefone yumvise azabanza kugaragara. Yagenzure mbere yo kohereza.”

**6. Emergency**
“Iyo ukeneye ubutabazi bwihuse, koresha nimero y'ubutabazi igaragara kuri IWACU. Kanda Hamagara 112 igihe ari byo bikenewe.”

**7. Privacy**
“Ushobora kwanga uruhushya rwa GPS cyangwa microphone. Ushobora gukomeza gukoresha ubundi buryo bwa serivisi butabukeneye.”

### Audio technical requirements
- Record with a fluent speaker after the scripts are reviewed by people who benefit from the guidance.
- Keep each clip short and focused on one action.
- Save a transcript that matches the recording exactly.
- Use a spoken Kinyarwanda voice only for Kinyarwanda clips. Never play English or French TTS as if it were Kinyarwanda.
- Provide pause, resume, replay, and stop controls.
- Audio is never automatic and never the only way to receive essential information.
- Make the transcript available even when audio is disabled, blocked, or unavailable.
- Do not upload identifiable voices or recordings without informed consent and an agreed retention/publication policy.

## Release gate

A media item can appear in the public site only after:
- written and recorded content is reviewed by relevant community members;
- consent and use rights are documented;
- all text, transcripts, and fallback instructions are available;
- the mobile and low-data experience has been checked;
- the media is accurately identified as Rwanda Sign Language or Kinyarwanda;
- the accessibility team records the reviewer names/roles and approval date.

Until then, the public guide must keep saying that RSL videos and human-recorded Kinyarwanda clips are being prepared, not that they are already available.
